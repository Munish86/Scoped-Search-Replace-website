Your host installed an SSL certificate and the site opens with https://. Yet some pages show no padlock, or the browser warns that parts of the page are "not secure". The cause is almost always old http:// addresses saved inside your content. Here is how to find and fix them.

What mixed content is

A page loaded over https that pulls in an image, script or stylesheet over plain http has mixed content. Browsers block or flag those parts, and the page loses its padlock. Ordinary links to http:// pages do not cause the warning, but they send visitors through an extra redirect, so it is worth fixing them too.

To see which addresses a page loads over http, open it, press F12 to open the browser's developer tools and look at the Console: each mixed-content item is listed with its address.

Step 1: back up and change the site address

Take a full database backup. Then under Settings › General, change WordPress Address and Site Address from http:// to https:// and save. This makes WordPress itself build https addresses from now on, but it does not change the addresses already saved in your posts.

Step 2: count the old addresses

In Scoped Search & Replace, choose the posts table, field Post/Page Content, All Content Types and All statuses, and search for your own address with http, for example:

http://www.example.com

Search exactly as the address is written. If your site was used both with and without www., search for both forms. Then repeat the search with the post meta table, where page builders and themes keep their links and background images.

Step 3: replace

In Find and Replace, set the same scope and replace http://www.example.com with https://www.example.com. Check the confirmation (old value, new value, table, field, scope) and click OK. Then do the same in post meta.

Replace your own domain only. Don't replace every http:// with https://: links to other websites that don't support https would break. Always include your domain in the old value.

Step 4: what is left

If the browser still warns after the replacement:

  • Clear caches: your caching plugin, your host's cache and, for builders, regenerate their CSS files.
  • Builder data with escaped slashes: some builders save URLs as http:\/\/www.example.com. Search post meta for http:\/\/www.example.com and replace it with https:\/\/www.example.com.
  • Widgets, Customizer and theme settings live in the options table, which the plugin doesn't change. Fix them in Appearance › Widgets and Appearance › Customize.
  • Theme or plugin files with a hard-coded http address: ask the developer for an update.
  • Images from other sites that only work over http: download them and add them to your own media library.

Finally, make sure every http address of your site redirects to https. Most hosts have a switch for this; otherwise ask them to add the redirect.
